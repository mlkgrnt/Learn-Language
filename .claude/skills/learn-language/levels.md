# CEFR Level Reference for Language Learning

## A1 — Beginner

**Vocabulary**: ~500 words
**Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Greetings and introductions
- Numbers, dates, time
- Family and relationships
- Colors, shapes, sizes
- Food and drink
- Daily routine
- Weather
- Basic directions (left, right, straight)
- Common objects (house, school, office)

### Grammar Points
- Subject pronouns (I, you, he, she...)
- Verb "to be" (am, is, are)
- Present simple tense (basic forms)
- Articles (a, an, the)
- Plural nouns
- Basic adjectives (big, small, good, bad)
- Possessive adjectives (my, your, his, her)
- Question words (what, where, when, who)
- Prepositions of place (in, on, at, under)
- Demonstratives (this, that, these, those)
- There is / There are
- Can / Can't for ability

### Skills
- Introduce yourself and others
- Ask and answer simple personal questions
- Order food and drink
- Ask for basic directions
- Tell time and dates
- Describe people and objects simply

---

## A2 — Elementary

**Vocabulary**: ~1,000 words
**Can understand sentences and frequently used expressions related to basic personal and family information, shopping, local geography, employment.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Hobbies and free time
- Shopping and money
- Travel and transport
- Health and body
- House and furniture
- Clothes and appearance
- Work and professions
- Feelings and emotions
- Common verbs of movement
- Instructions and directions

### Grammar Points
- Past simple (regular and common irregular verbs)
- Future with "going to" and "will"
- Present continuous
- Comparative and superlative adjectives
- Adverbs of frequency
- Conjunctions (and, but, because, so)
- Modal verbs (must, should, have to)
- Countable and uncountable nouns
- Some / any / much / many
- Phrasal verbs (basic: get up, turn on, look for)
- Prepositions of time (in, on, at)
- Basic conditionals (if + present)

### Skills
- Describe past events and experiences
- Make simple plans and predictions
- Go shopping and handle transactions
- Give and follow directions
- Describe your home and neighborhood
- Talk about habits and routines

---

## B1 — Intermediate

**Vocabulary**: ~2,500 words
**Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Education and learning
- Environment and nature
- Technology and media
- Entertainment and culture
- Social issues
- Travel and tourism (detailed)
- Food and cooking
- Sports and fitness
- News and current events
- Idioms and common expressions

### Grammar Points
- Present perfect simple and continuous
- Past continuous
- Used to / would for past habits
- Passive voice (all basic tenses)
- Reported speech (statements and questions)
- First and second conditionals
- Relative clauses (who, which, that, where)
- Gerunds and infinitives
- Modal verbs for deduction (must be, might be, can't be)
- Connectors (however, although, despite, in spite of)
- Reported questions
- Quantifiers (enough, too, rather, quite)

### Skills
- Narrate a story or experience
- Express and justify opinions
- Understand the main points of news articles
- Write formal and informal letters/emails
- Participate in discussions on familiar topics
- Deal with most travel situations

---

## B2 — Upper Intermediate

**Vocabulary**: ~4,000 words
**Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Business and finance
- Politics and society
- Science and research
- Abstract concepts (justice, freedom, success)
- Collocations and word families
- Formal vs. informal register
- Phrasal verbs (advanced)
- Academic vocabulary
- Idiomatic expressions
- Colloquialisms and slang

### Grammar Points
- All conditional forms (zero, first, second, third, mixed)
- Future perfect and future continuous
- Past perfect simple and continuous
- Advanced passive constructions
- Wish / if only
- Inversion (rarely do, not only...but also)
- Cleft sentences (What I mean is..., It was John who...)
- Advanced relative clauses (whose, whom, preposition + which)
- Participle clauses
- Discourse markers (nevertheless, furthermore, consequently)
- Subjunctive mood (suggest that he be...)
- Advanced modal perfect (must have, should have, could have)

### Skills
- Understand complex arguments and texts
- Write clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects
- Participate fluently in conversations with native speakers
- Present arguments with supporting evidence
- Understand films and TV without major difficulty
- Write essays and reports

---

## C1 — Advanced

**Vocabulary**: ~6,000+ words
**Can understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts, and recognize implicit meaning.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Specialized terminology by field
- Nuanced synonyms and antonyms
- Connotation and register awareness
- Literary and rhetorical devices
- Advanced idioms and proverbs
- Etymology and word formation
- Euphemisms and diplomatic language
- Technical and professional jargon

### Grammar Points
- Advanced tense usage and aspect
- Nuanced modal usage
- Advanced participle constructions
- Nominalization
- Hedging and boosting language
- Advanced discourse management
- Stylistic inversion
- Ellipsis and substitution
- Advanced reported speech
- Emphatic structures (do/does/did emphasis)

### Skills
- Understand implicit meaning in complex texts
- Express yourself fluently and spontaneously
- Use language flexibly for social, academic, professional purposes
- Produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects
- Understand humor, irony, and cultural references

---

## C2 — Mastery

**Vocabulary**: ~8,000+ words
**Can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read.**

### Vocabulary Themes
- Full native-level vocabulary range
- Archaic and literary terms
- Regional dialects and variations
- Slang evolution and neologisms
- Cross-register vocabulary

### Grammar Points
- Full command of all grammatical structures
- Stylistic choices for effect
- Error-free writing at native level
- Understanding of prescriptive vs. descriptive grammar
- Ability to explain grammar rules to others

### Skills
- Summarize information from different spoken and written sources
- Express yourself spontaneously, very fluently and precisely
- Differentiate finer shades of meaning even in complex situations
- Write publishable-quality text
- Understand any accent or dialect

## Special Notes by Language Family

Adapt your teaching approach based on the user's L1 (native language) and target language combination. Here are common challenges by language pair:

### For learners of languages with articles (English, French, German, Spanish, etc.) from languages without articles (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, etc.)
- Focus heavily on articles (a/an/the, le/la, der/die/das) — these don't exist in the learner's L1
- Tense/aspect systems differ significantly — explicit comparison helps

### For learners of East Asian languages (Japanese, Korean, Chinese)
- Writing system is a major focus area at early levels (Hiragana/Katakana, Hangul, characters)
- Particles/postpositions require extensive practice
- Honorific/politeness systems are a core challenge

### For learners of Romance/Germanic languages
- Gender systems (masculine/feminine/neuter) require attention for learners from genderless languages
- Verb conjugation is a major focus area
- Subjunctive mood appears at B1-B2

**General rule**: When you learn the user's L1 and target language combination, research and apply the specific contrastive analysis for that pair. The examples above are starting points — always adapt to the actual combination.
